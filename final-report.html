What happened with release 4.2,
and how we stop it happening again

Six sessions of work in one story: what broke, how we found out, the fix we would build, and the habit that keeps the next release honest.

Rook is a fictional company Course project

The answer, first

A shorter timer starved four responders. We fix it in two steps

What happened

4.2 cut the time to answer a ping from 90s to 60s. Missed pings went from 2% to 21.5%. Four responders now miss 59% of pings and cannot climb back.

The fix

Step 1, this release: restore 90s and reset the four scores.
Step 2, in 4.3: scoring that can forgive, plus a handler timeline and a responder countdown.

The decision

Helen: ship step 1 alone, put the scoring fix in 4.3 scope, and send the note to handlers once step 1 is scheduled.

The rest of this report is the proof: six sessions of findings, the exact prompts that produced them, a prototype you can click, and the review habit that keeps it from happening again.

In plain words

4.2 made responders miss pings, and then punished them for it

On 12 August we cut the time a responder has to answer a ping from 90 seconds to 60, and shifted the ranking toward who is nearest. Missed pings jumped that same day, and the way the ranking is built means some responders could not recover.

Why am I not getting pings?

Two changes shipped together

  • Ping wait: 90s to 60s.
  • Ranking weights: nearness 0.45 to 0.60, recent acceptance 0.40 to 0.25.

Who it hit

Four responders (The Undertow, Farlight, Meteor Mite, Vesper) lost 65 to 74% of their pings. Two others gained about 45%. Total pings stayed flat: the work moved, it did not disappear.

Issue #4 · X-Ray Vision · prompt 7

The code only ever lets a yes raise a score

I asked
Find me the part of this code that takes points off somebody when they miss a ping or turn one down. Show it to me and explain it in plain English. Then find me every single thing in this code that puts points back on.
Finding
+0.08
A yes
the only credit, in one place (offer.py)
-0.12
A miss or a turn-down
same penalty (record_declined)
  • A score only rises if more than 60% of pings are taken (0.6 × 0.08 = 0.4 × 0.12).
  • No decay, no reset. A quiet responder cannot recover on their own.
  • A miss and a turn-down are recorded separately, then scored the same.
  • The four hit hardest had ordinary acceptance before 4.2 (68.7 to 81.9%), so the change did not target people already turning jobs down.

What the prototype stands on

The prototype shows it. Two steps actually fix it

A prototype changes no wait and no ranking. The fix is the change underneath, in this order.

Step 1 · this release
  • Restore the 90 second wait.
  • Reset only the four affected scores.
  • Tell Supply first, because callout load shifts.
  • Watch for two weeks.

In my toy model (invented responders), 90s alone cuts misses from 19% to 4%. Real data has to confirm it.

Step 2 · 4.3
  • Forgiving scoring: split a miss from a turn-down, ease scores toward neutral, one fair ping after a gap.
  • Handler timeline and re-offer.
  • Responder countdown and a plain message after a miss.
  • No scores shown to anyone, only counts.

How we would know (targets are proposals, not measured)

Missed rate under 4% within 2 weeks (now 12.7%)
The four under 10% missed, pings back within 20% of normal
45 open tickets to 0, uncovered callouts back to about 5%

Strategy

Why step 1 first, and what it costs

Principle: never penalise a responder for something the system caused.

The business case
  • Revenue is a subscription per active responder, so a responder who has gone quiet is a responder at risk.
  • The size of that risk is unknown. The wiki and the handoff have no churn data. I would ask Helen who owns churn analysis.
  • Doing nothing costs: four responders stay starved, 45 tickets stay open, and callouts keep moving on. Pings per callout already rose from 1.23 to 1.39.
Trade-offs we accept
  • Slower handoff: 90 seconds means up to 30 seconds longer before the next responder is offered an unanswered callout.
  • Something gives in 4.3: I suggest Availability Confidence, which was committed for 4.2 and never shipped.
  • Supply: maintenance windows shift when callouts move, so we tell them first.

How my skill keeps this from happening again

Every brief has to name who is watching

The checklist does not make a design choice right. It makes sure someone owns the number that would show a choice was wrong, before it ships.
