### 7.

I got an argument for you.  What should I do?

Why I'd still lead with unanswered callouts

* A missed ping isn't necessarily a failure. If the next responder takes the callout, the only cost is some delay. ELT cares about callouts that didn't get covered, and missed pings can rise without that happening.
* The missed rate partly changes by definition. A 60-second wait will record more misses than a 90-second one even if responders behave exactly the same. It's a good diagnostic, but it can look worse or better than the real outcome.
* It's a mechanism. Presenting it first invites a debate about the wait setting, when the question for ELT is whether handlers are getting coverage.

### 8.

another argument 

Why unfilled callouts is weaker for this problem

* Your Module 1 notes put the unfilled callouts in Southport, Riverside and Kingsbridge, not in the starved responders' areas. That suggests a different problem, probably coverage. Using it as the success metric for a ping-timing fix could show no improvement even if the fix works, or show a change we didn't cause.
* It is a lagging, diluted measure. A callout stays filled if the next responder takes it, so pings can fail repeatedly with no visible effect on this number. It is also less sensitive week to week, because far fewer callouts than pings happen.
